# Coverage and review: choosing a study set

This original self-hosted example teaches how to choose and build study forms from the seven sections of [source.md](source.md). It does not claim coverage of an external subject. [source-map.json](source-map.json) records source relationships and internal native target keys, not a manuscript adapter.

## Authored forms

- [Notes](notes.tex), with explanation in [notes-content.tex](notes-content.tex)
- [Unified Quick Reference](quick-reference.tex), with substantive entries and direct routes in [entries.tex](entries.tex)
- [Optional Keyword Index](keyword-index.tex), a compact view of the same registry
- [Decision Tree](decision-tree.tex), using the reader graph API in [decisions.tex](decisions.tex)

English is on the physical left and Simplified Chinese on the right. Common presentation lives in [common.tex](common.tex) and [languages.tex](languages.tex). The [Makefile](Makefile) selects products and builds included notes first. The default pair is notes plus Quick Reference; standalone forms omit unavailable companion imports.

The lookup contains five canonical concepts, three subentries and twelve alias/keyword route records. The graph contains two questions and four substantive solutions. Its declarations generate reader numbers N1–N6 independently of their internal semantic keys. These are source inventories, not page counts.

## Coverage

S1 is represented by the four forms and both entry questions. S2 is explained in notes-evidence and the evidence/coverage/source-map routes, and applied in the explanatory-notes solution. S3 appears in build and portable-project and the solution approaches. S4 appears in quick, aliases/find-word/qr and the lookup solutions. S5 appears in notes-flow, page-breaks and the explanatory-notes solution. S6 appears in tree-clarification, tree-unknown and the two questions plus the decision-authoring solution. S7 appears in review, evidence and the final decision-authoring checks. Exact keys are in the source map.

The forms share coverage rather than repeating every source sentence. The index intentionally omits explanatory bodies. The decision example teaches this source's actual task, building study forms; its solutions provide the authoring approach rather than stopping at a form name.

## Retrieval exercises

1. QR reaches the Quick Reference canonical entry and its notes section when included.
2. Find a word offers direct, context-labeled compact-answer and location-only targets.
3. Page breaks reaches the Long paragraphs subentry's own page.
4. Coverage and Source map reach Scope and evidence, distinguishing provenance from manuscript input.
5. Missing information and Unknown reach the decision entry's explanation of inline clarification and substantive information-gathering routes.
6. Decision Tree shares one headword with its canonical entry; it creates no redundant lookup section.
7. The standalone index retains local concept/subentry targets and omits missing companion links.

## Decision exercises

- N1 A → N3: explanation, assumptions, worked application and source checks
- N1 B → N2 A → N4: one canonical answer registry and direct lookup routes
- N1 B → N2 B → N5: exact locations in delivered target documents
- N1 C → N6: observable questions, first-match conditions and executable solutions

Resolve an unclear task at N1 and unclear retrieval expectations at N2 before choosing. These prerequisites are inline; obtaining them does not need a separate inspection-and-return node. Each solution answers its selected authoring task without a redundant completion node. Multiple requested forms are handled by applying the question to each real task. Other subjects may need substantive information-gathering nodes or genuine progress-bearing cycles.

## Delivery and limits

Rebuild changed forms, verify the actual links and inspect every changed page before publishing. Keep selected PDFs beside their relative companion targets. Deliver the editable native project with source map, dependency assets and licenses. Report passes, failures and unrun checks distinctly. This example does not demonstrate every language, printer or PDF viewer.
