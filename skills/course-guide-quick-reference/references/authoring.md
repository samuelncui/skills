# From sources to notes and retrieval

## Source map

Use a lightweight table or data file with: source identifier, exact page/slide/section, concept ID, intended notes section, and coverage status (covered, partial, missing or deliberately excluded with a reason). Reconcile the ledger against the full authorized source inventory, not only the headings selected for the notes. Give newly authored examples their own label rather than pretending they came from the source. Preserve provenance in the private working project; publish it only when authorized and appropriate.

Read diagrams as well as extracted text. A slide heading, unexplained symbol or broken OCR token is not enough to reconstruct a claim confidently. Mark a gap, consult authorized context or ask a focused question. Do not remove uncertainty simply to make the notes look finished.

## Teaching notes

Use the structure that fits the concept. A definition may need a contrast and example; a procedure may need inputs, preconditions, steps and failure cases; a mathematical result may need notation, assumptions, derivation and a worked calculation. Avoid forcing all three into identical fields.

Explain the context, why the idea matters and what the reader can do after the section. For mechanisms and failure cases, explain the cause rather than merely naming the outcome. Include the conditions that make an application valid. Resolve notation before using it. Work through the intermediate steps that the source omits when they are soundly derivable, and identify them as explanation. Check examples numerically and dimensionally. Do not turn source-specific administrative directions into universal learning rules.

Make examples self-contained: state the inputs, units, assumptions, relevant diagram and complete reasoning needed to reach the result. Avoid dangling “as above” or “see slide” dependencies.

Every referenced figure must actually appear or have an explicit, accurate locator in the delivered bundle. Explain what to read in it and why it supports the claim; a caption alone may not teach the diagram. For figures, preserve the information needed to support the prose. An original diagram may replace a source figure when it communicates the same authorized idea, but it must not silently alter relationships, scale or labels. Inspect actual pixels; a successful compile will not detect a misleading drawing.

## Quick Reference: keywords lead to concepts

Maintain one canonical explanation per concept ID. An entry should answer a useful recall or decision question, not repeat the entire notes section. Choose only useful elements, such as a plain-language meaning, formula with conditions, discriminating example, procedure, or important limitation.

Aliases and acronyms point to the canonical entry. Secondary keywords form an inverted lookup: a remembered word or task can point to several concepts without duplicating their entries. Prefer the reader's search vocabulary; include localized variants where helpful. Sorting may use the source-language headword, a supplied sort key, or a task grouping. State the choice; an English A–Z index is not a universal multilingual convention. In an alphabetical scheme, secondary words need their own alias/keyword rows under their own sort positions, or a real secondary index. “Capacity; overflow” under C alone does not provide an O lookup. Redirect these rows to the same canonical body rather than duplicating it.

Keep local entry links distinct from notes links. Local references answer “which concept?”; notes section/page pointers answer “where is the fuller explanation?” Give both routes a visible section/entry identifier and generated page when the material must work in print. A clickable title alone is insufficient for printed lookup; a chapter-only pointer is too broad when a precise entry is available. Use native labels and generated page references. Rebuild dependent documents after editing notes.

## Language decisions

Read a full paragraph or worked example before translating individual phrases. Keep a small project terminology list for recurring concepts, symbols, aliases and deliberate variants; consistency must not turn into unnatural word-for-word phrasing. Read each language on its own, including English when present, for clear complete reasoning. Then compare the pair for the same claim, conditions, emphasis, uncertainty and result. A fluent translation can still omit a qualification.

## Two useful checks

1. Choose a concept and verify that every important claim can be traced to a source, a clearly labeled derivation, or an explicitly original example.
2. Start with an alias or practical keyword rather than a chapter title. Can a reader reach the right concept and then its explanation without guessing?
